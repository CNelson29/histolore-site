## A Civilization Older Than Memory

Long before Earth's first cities rose from the dust, before any human hand carved stone into monument, an extraordinarily advanced race had already crossed galaxies, built wonders beyond imagination, and then — bafflingly — vanished. In the world of *Stargate*, this race is known as the Ancients. Their story stretches across millions of years and multiple galaxies, threading through plague, exile, genetic legacy, and a form of evolution so radical it left the physical universe behind entirely. Understanding what happened to them is, in many ways, understanding the entire hidden history of the *Stargate* universe.

## Who Were the Ancients? The Alteran Origins

The Ancients were not always called by that name. In their earliest recorded history, according to the mythology developed across *Stargate SG-1* and *Stargate: Atlantis*, they were known as the Altera — an advanced race of humans originating in a distant galaxy far from the Milky Way. They were among the most scientifically gifted civilizations ever to exist, possessed of technology that made them effectively gods by any human standard.

Their society, however, was not without deep internal fracture. At some point in the remote past, the Alteran community split along a fundamental philosophical fault line. One faction pushed for a religious framework — a system of belief and worship that would eventually define the Ori, the Ancients' estranged cousins. A smaller, scientifically-minded group refused to follow that path. Facing what the *Stargate* mythology frames as the threat of persecution, this minority made a decisive choice: they built ships and left, departing for the Milky Way galaxy. They took with them their science, their culture, and an unquenchable drive to understand the universe — leaving behind the Ori, who would later become one of the most dangerous forces in the franchise.

## Builders of the Stargates

Upon arriving in the Milky Way, the Alterans — increasingly known simply as the Ancients — began an era of extraordinary creation. Their most celebrated achievement was the Stargate network: an interconnected system of ring-shaped portals capable of transporting matter instantly across interstellar distances. As established in *Stargate SG-1* from its second season onward (the episode "The Fifth Race" first described the Ancients as the original builders and founding members of the Alliance of Four Great Races), this network eventually spanned multiple galaxies, including the Pegasus galaxy featured in *Stargate: Atlantis*.

The Stargates were not merely engineering marvels — they were an expression of the Ancients' larger ambition. They seeded life across uninhabited worlds throughout multiple galaxies, allowing evolution to unfold on countless planets. In this way, they became the indirect ancestors of many humanoid species scattered across the stars. Their floating capital city, Atlantis, constructed in the Pegasus galaxy, represented the pinnacle of their physical achievement: a technological wonder on a scale nothing in human history can approximate.

## Plague, Retreat, and a World Left Behind

Despite their extraordinary capabilities, the Ancients proved vulnerable to forces far smaller than themselves. A devastating plague swept through their Milky Way civilization — one they could not cure despite all their accumulated knowledge. The *Stargate* lore does not specify the exact nature of the disease with precision, and the sources treat its details as uncertain, but its consequences were catastrophic and civilization-ending.

Forced from their settlements, some Ancients eventually returned to Earth — roughly 10,000 years ago, according to the mythology established across the franchise. But this was not the Earth they had once known. Human civilization had developed independently during their absence, and the returning Ancients found themselves strangers. Some chose to live among these early humans, interbreeding with them over generations. This is the origin of what researchers in the show call the "Ancient gene" — a genetic marker still carried by a small percentage of modern humans, which uniquely allows its bearers to interface with and activate Ancient technology. Others could not or would not reintegrate into the physical world at all.

## Ascension: Evolution Beyond the Body

The most defining element of the Ancients' ultimate fate is what the *Stargate* franchise calls Ascension. Rather than dying in the conventional sense, many Ancients achieved — or chose — a transformation so complete that it defies ordinary description: they shed their physical forms entirely, evolving into beings of pure energy existing on a higher plane of reality.

Ascended Ancients are immensely powerful, capable of perceiving and influencing the physical universe in ways that appear supernatural to lower-plane beings. However, they collectively adopted a strict non-interference policy — a binding agreement against directly intervening in the lives or conflicts of those still existing in the physical realm. This group, known as "the Others," creates one of the franchise's deepest ongoing tensions: beings of almost limitless power who observe but rarely act, watching civilizations rise and fall without lifting a metaphorical finger. Not every Ascended Ancient adhered rigidly to this code, and those who broke the rules faced consequences from their peers — a recurring source of moral drama across both series.

## Legacy, the Ori, and What Was Left Behind

The Ancients did not leave the universe quietly. Their technology — the Stargates, the city of Atlantis, countless weapons and devices — became the most fought-over prize in the galaxy. The Goa'uld, a parasitic alien species, exploited the Stargate network for centuries to build their own empire across hundreds of worlds, passing themselves off as gods to the human populations they enslaved.

The Ori, meanwhile — the Ancients' religious counterparts who had remained in their original galaxy — eventually emerged as a direct threat. Unlike the Ancients, the Ori demanded active worship from lower beings and fed on that devotion as a form of energy. The contrast between these two branches of the same ancestral species became the moral center of *Stargate SG-1*'s later seasons: one group transcended and withdrew; the other transcended and demanded. Another of the ancient Alliance races, the Asgard, eventually gifted all of their accumulated knowledge and technology to the humans of Earth before their own civilization ended — an act that echoed the Ancients' own complicated legacy of leaving gifts without staying to explain them.

## Why It Still Matters

The story of the Ancients resonates because it asks genuinely difficult questions through a science-fiction lens: What do great civilizations actually leave behind? What does it mean to evolve so far beyond the ordinary that you can no longer fully connect with those you once were? What responsibilities do the vastly powerful have toward those who inherit their world?

The Ancients built the roads of a galaxy and then disappeared, leaving their maps and monuments for younger civilizations to stumble upon, misuse, and slowly begin to understand. Entire empires rose and fell in the ruins of their achievement. Their ascension was not an escape — it was a transformation that came with its own ethical constraints and its own kinds of failure. In the *Stargate* universe, they are simultaneously the ultimate ancestors and the ultimate absentee guardians: everywhere in their legacy, absent when their presence might matter most. That is a story human history knows well.
